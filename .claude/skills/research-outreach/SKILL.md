## Purpose
Turn a lead sheet into conversations. Produce public post variants, a direct-message script, a give-first asset, rules of engagement, and a small experiment to find what earns useful replies.

## Input
**Works best with:** output of `community-discovery` (map, themes, lead sheet, community rules).
**Also useful:** the single question you most need answered, and what you can offer back.

## Key Concepts
These are behavioral heuristics, not proven laws. Treat each as an experiment on your audience.
| Principle | Use |
|---|---|
| Correction beats questions | Post a specific draft claim and ask what is wrong. |
| War stories carry status | Ask for the worst or funniest instance. |
| Low reply effort | One concrete question, answerable in a sentence. |
| Specific numbers invite correction | State an assumed number; let people fix it. |
| Honest learner framing | "Researching, not selling." Say it only if true. |
| Reciprocity | Give a useful artifact before asking. |
| Shared enemy | Aim at the problem, not at a product. |

**Ethics floor:** disclose who you are; never pose as a customer; do not link a product you have not built; follow each community's self-promotion rules; keep DMs few and personal; share results back; honor "no" instantly.

## Application
1. **Pick one question** that decides a hypothesis (e.g., hours per instance, who owns it, what happened to the deal).
2. **Read the rules** for each target community; note if a research post is allowed.
3. **Write 3 post variants**: correction, war-story, number-anchor. No product links.
4. **Prepare a give-first asset**: a checklist, template, or anonymized summary of findings.
5. **Write the DM script** (under 80 words): who you are, which of their words you are referencing, one small question, easy exit.
6. **Set limits**: at most 5 DMs a day; no follow-up beyond one polite nudge.
7. **Run the experiment**: post variants across at least 2 communities; record replies, first-hand replies, interview opt-ins, stated numbers.
8. **Close the loop**: thank repliers, post the summary of findings, keep a status per lead (contacted, replied, opted in, declined).
9. **Decide**: if first-hand replies are under the bar you set, rework the question before contacting more people.

### Output template
```markdown
## Research question
## Community rules check

## Give-first asset

## Experiment log
| Variant | Community | Replies | First-hand | Opt-ins | Notes |

| Handle | Status | Date | Next step |
```

## Common Pitfalls
- Disguised pitches; communities and people spot them fast.
- Bulk-messaging everyone on the lead sheet.
- Asking vague questions ("thoughts?").
- Not sharing results back, which makes it extraction.
- Interpreting upvotes as validation; count first-hand answers instead.
