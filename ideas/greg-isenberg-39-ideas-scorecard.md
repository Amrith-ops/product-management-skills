## Pick from the founder's point of view
**Best fit: #16 Geek Squad for AI, but only as a productized, vertical offer (18/24).**
- Matches the founder's priorities: small and family-owned businesses, low-tech buyers who cannot do it themselves, reachable in owner groups, no field work, USD retainers.
- Pricing evidence: done-for-you AI agent setup is sold as a one-time setup plus a monthly retainer; sources show roughly $300-$1,500/month tiers and $500-$2,000 setup (consultant and vendor claims, unverified). That sits in the $400-650/month range needed for 30-50 clients.
- Risks: it is labor, not software (hurts margin and the 9-5); many AI-automation agencies already sell this; agent access to client accounts creates security and liability risk.
- Fix: do not sell "AI setup" generically. Sell **one repeatable workflow to one vertical**. The parked idea (AI order entry for small distributors) is exactly that, so it stays the lead.

**Runner-up: #33 Jiffy Lube for vibe-coded apps (18/24).**
- Strong on urgency (a broken app is hair on fire), group reach (vibe-coder communities), and buildability.
- Weak on fit with a 9-5: fixes arrive as tickets. Rescue services already exist at every price from about $30-100 fixed fixes to $1.5-8k packages and $15k+ agency rescues (vendor figures). A productized fixed-price tier plus a maintenance plan could work.

**Not for this founder:** #3, #7, #15, #17, #19, #23, #25, #32 (capital, regulation, hardware, field work, or marketplace cold start).

## Notes
- Most agent-infrastructure ideas (#2, #4, #8, #9, #27, #31) target technical buyers who can self-serve, and the market is early; they score low on "less tech-savvy" and "hair on fire".
- Consumer ideas (#13, #14, #22, #24, #36, #37) score low on "few clients, high revenue".
