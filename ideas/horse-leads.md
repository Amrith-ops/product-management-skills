# Idea check: horse buying and selling leads for elite buyers
Date: 2026-10-24. Proposed by the founder (reasoning: elite buyers and high ticket prices imply high margins). Checked with one round of web searches (trade articles, forums and law-firm commentary; mostly anecdotal). 🔶 assumption; 🔵 open question.

## Verdict
**Not recommended. Score about 13 of 24.** The high margin in horse sales belongs to the broker or agent, not to a lead seller, and being the agent brings trust, legal and field-work problems.

## What is right in the intuition
- High ticket sizes; few clients could produce large revenue.
- Trainers and agents typically take about 10% commission (forum reports 7.5%-15%): [Practical Horseman](https://practicalhorseman.com/how-does-the-horse-sale-commission-system-work-11701/), [Chronicle of the Horse forum](https://forum.chronofhorse.com/t/sales-commission/333026).
- Buyers can be low-tech and relationship-driven.

## What does not hold
| Issue | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Lead and listing prices are low | Listing sites charge about $10-$30 per ad: Equine.com about $20 for 120 days, Dreamhorse $20 photo ad, Horseclicks $10-$20, Horse & Hound GBP 29.99 ([Equine Network](https://equinenetwork.com/equine-com-simplifies-horse-sales-with-newly-redesigned-site/), [Mississippi State Extension](https://extension.msstate.edu/publications/new-era-marketing-horses-for-sale)). Some prices are dated (2020). |
| The commission goes to the agent | To earn it you would have to act as broker, with duties of full disclosure and acting in the client's interest ([Barrel Horse News](https://barrelhorsenews.com/uncategorized/legal-disputes-in-horse-sales/)). |
| Trust is the product | Many sales are sealed by verbal agreement; secret commissions and dual agency are recurring disputes; some states (Florida, California, Kentucky) regulate horse sales ([Plaid Horse](https://www.theplaidhorse.com/2019/05/31/issues-surrounding-commission-payments/), Practical Horseman). |
| Fraud risk | Fake listings with forged ownership papers and upfront payment requests ([The Horse](https://thehorse.com/18928/10-horse-sales-fraud-warning-signs/)). |
| Field work | Pre-purchase vet exams, x-rays and trials are part of serious sales; a high-end UK site requires a vet report with x-rays and a GBP 50,000 minimum price ([Racing Post/Goffs coverage](https://www.thoroughbreddailynews.com/goffs-launches-instant-online-platform/)). |
| Elite buyers use trusted trainers | Reaching them needs relationships and reputation; the founder has neither and no domain knowledge. 🔵 |
| Not urgent | Discretionary purchase; no hair-on-fire pain found. |
