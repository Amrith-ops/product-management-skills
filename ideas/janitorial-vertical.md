# Chosen vertical: janitorial and sanitation supply ("jan-san") distributors
Date: 2026-10-24. Picked by the assistant because the founder has no domain knowledge and asked for a recommendation. Part of `ideas/distributor-order-desk.md`.
Legend: ✔ supported by something I found; 🔶 my assumption or general knowledge; 🔵 open question to check with owners.

## Why this vertical (for a founder starting blank)
| Reason | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Many small, family-run distributors | ✔ trade coverage describes the industry as highly fragmented and gives family-owned examples ([profile](https://www.fundinguniverse.com/company-histories/national-sanitary-supply-co-history/), [CleanLink](https://www.cleanlink.com/sm/article/A-Closer-Look-At-Buying-Groups--19751)). The fragmentation claim is from a mid-1990s profile, so 🔵 confirm it still holds. |
| A group channel exists | ✔ buying groups gather distributors: one example, DPA Buying Group, describes 1,100+ distributors and 220 suppliers across janitorial, industrial, safety, packaging and restoration. Trade media exist (CleanLink / Sanitary Maintenance). |
| Customer-specific pricing and recurring customers | ✔ vendors describe contract pricing, rebates, high SKU counts and recurring customers ([MindHARBOR](https://www.mindharbor.com/distribution-manufacturing-industries-served/janitorial-sanitation-erp-integrations)). This makes learned per-customer mappings (the data moat) valuable. |
| Manual order entry takes rep time | ✔ vendor/trade statement that reps pushing orders through manually cannot engage customers ([Profit Optics](https://www.profitoptics.com/insights/blog/the-upside-to-tackling-the-downsides-of-order-entry-for-distributors)). 🔵 No data found on how many orders arrive by phone, text or email. |
| Easier to learn than plumbing, industrial or foodservice | 🔶 consumables (paper, liners, chemicals, gloves, dispensers) reorder on repeat; fewer technical part numbers than plumbing or industrial. |
| Foodservice is already targeted by funded vendors | ✔ Pepper and Cut+Dry focus on food distribution. 🔵 I did not find an AI order-entry vendor focused on jan-san; check. |
| No regulatory exposure for order entry itself | 🔶 you handle orders, not product safety data. |

**Alternatives I checked and set aside:** plumbing/HVAC supply (I found no evidence on supply-house order-entry pain; deeper part-number knowledge needed) and foodservice (funded specialists).
**Main risks:** thin margins may limit price tolerance; ERPs built for jan-san exist (Epicor Prophet 21, Klipboard, Advantive, Aptean Merlin), so owners may say "our ERP does that" (it handles entry, not messy intake); big-box and online competition; 🔵 unknown share of orders arriving by phone, text or email.

## Jan-san 101 (🔶 general knowledge; confirm with a few calls)
- **Products:** paper towels and tissue, trash liners (sized like 40x46), cleaning chemicals, floor-care, gloves, dispensers, restroom and kitchen items.
- **Units:** each, pack, case; the same item can be ordered in different units.
- **Customers:** building service contractors (cleaning companies), schools, offices, healthcare facilities, restaurants and small businesses.
- **Pricing:** often customer-specific or contract pricing; rebates through buying groups.
- **Words to learn:** SKU, case pack, contract price, buying group, stock vs special order, drop-ship, route delivery, standing (recurring) order.

## How to reach them without contacts
**1. LinkedIn (cold, track A).** Search people: titles "customer service manager", "inside sales manager", "operations manager", "owner", "president" with keywords "janitorial", "sanitary supply", "jan-san", "cleaning supplies distributor"; location United States; company size 11-200. Save 30 to a sheet.
**2. Warm introductions (track B).** ERP consultants and resellers serving jan-san distributors (the vendors above have partner networks); trade media editors; buying-group member-services staff. Ask each for 2 introductions.
**3. Trade media and groups.** Read CleanLink and similar publications for distributor names and recurring problems; note buying groups and their events.
**4. Design partner offer.** Free first month to one distributor for access to real orders and feedback.

**First message (under 80 words):**
> Hi [Name], I'm researching how janitorial supply distributors handle customer orders that arrive by text, email and phone. I'm not selling anything. Roughly how many orders a day come in that way, and does someone retype them into your system? I'm happy to share what I learn from other distributors.

## Questions for the first 8 conversations
1. How many orders arrive each day, and by which channel (phone, text, email, portal, fax)?
2. Who enters them, and how long does one order take?
3. What happens when an order is wrong: how often, and what does it cost (credits, returns, lost customer)?
4. What system do you use today, and what does it not do?
5. How do you handle customer-specific pricing and recurring orders?
6. What have you tried (portals, offshore help, software), and why did it fail or stick?
7. Who decides about software spending, and what would a fair monthly price be?
8. Would you send us a week of anonymized orders to test accuracy?

## Reach-test gate (days 1-4)
Pass: at least 5 replies out of 20 cold messages and 3 agreeing to talk, or 2 warm introductions from track B. Fail: switch to trades owners (see `ideas/DECISION.md`).
