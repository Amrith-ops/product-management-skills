# Step 1 - Frame the problem
Skills: `problem-statement`, `proto-persona`, `jobs-to-be-done`

## Problem framing narrative
**I am:** a founder/CTO of a 15-person B2B SaaS closing our first mid-market and enterprise deals.
- I own security by default; there is no CISO.
- Sales ping me every time a prospect sends a questionnaire.
- I have policies and a pitch deck, but no single source of truth.

**Trying to:** answer the buyer's security review fast and accurately so the deal closes this quarter.

**But:**
- Each questionnaire has different wording for the same 50 underlying questions.
- Past answers live in old spreadsheets, Slack, and my head.
- The deadline is set by the buyer, and sales is chasing me daily.

**Because:** security knowledge is unstructured and every questionnaire is a one-off format, so reuse is manual.

**Which makes me feel:** interrupted, anxious about giving a wrong answer, and guilty that product work is slipping.

## Context and constraints
US/UK/EU, 5-50 employees, selling to companies that run vendor-risk reviews. 🔶 Often pre-SOC 2 or just starting it.

## Final problem statement
Small SaaS teams without a security function need to answer buyer security questionnaires in minutes with provable accuracy, because manual copy-paste takes 8-20 hours per questionnaire and stalls revenue.

## Proto-persona: Founder-CTO Frankie
- 30-45, US/UK/EU, Series Seed to A, 8-40 staff, technical, also acts as head of security.
- Quotes: "I just need to get past their security review." / "I don't know which answer we gave last time." / "I can't hand this to sales; they'll guess."
- Pains: lost hours, inconsistent answers, fear of misrepresenting controls.
- Goals: unblock deals, look credible, avoid buying an enterprise compliance suite yet.
- Decision authority: yes, can swipe a card up to ~$500/mo. Influencers: sales lead, auditors, advisors. 🔶

Secondary persona: Sales engineer / head of sales ops at a 30-100 person SaaS (owns the deal, needs the answer).

## Jobs-to-be-done
- Functional: complete the questionnaire; keep answers consistent; produce evidence links.
- Social: look like a mature vendor; not be "the bottleneck".
- Emotional: stop dreading each new questionnaire; trust that nothing false was submitted.
- Pains: hours per questionnaire, inconsistent answers, no audit trail.
- Gains: first draft in minutes, every answer cited to a policy, reuse across buyers.
- Unresolved by current tools: compliance suites are priced and scoped for later-stage companies; RFP tools target large sales teams. 🔶
